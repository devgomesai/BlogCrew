On May 15, 2026, India's Chief Justice Surya Kant called unemployed social media activists 'cockroaches' [2]. It backfired. Gen Z quickly turned the insult into a movement, arguing that like cockroaches, they can survive whatever political fallout the establishment throws at them [2, 3].

## The Genesis of the Cockroach Janta Party

### Reclaiming an Elitist Slur
Why cockroaches? Look at where Delhi's civil services aspirants live: cramped, illegal, windowless basements in coaching hubs like Old Rajinder Nagar and Mukherjee Nagar [3]. When Chief Justice Kant compared them to pests, aspirants saw it as pure contempt [2, 3]. The insect became a funny symbol of survival in a system that ignores them [3].

### Bypassing Traditional Channels with AI
On May 16, 2026, Dalit student Abhijeet Dipke and YouTuber Meghnad S. used AI to build a joke website [1, 5]. They called it the Cockroach Janta Party, or CJP [1, 5]. In two weeks, their Instagram page hit 20 million followers, and soon reached 25 million, beating the digital reach of both the BJP and the main opposition [4, 5]. The digital joke quickly turned into real, coordinated protests on the streets [5].

## Traditional Campus Unions vs. The Horizontal Alternative

### The Complicity of Campus Politics
Regular students feel disconnected from big campus unions like the right-wing ABVP or Congress-aligned NSUI [7]. Student politics runs on cash, muscle, and caste, leaving student welfare as an afterthought [7]. In a crisis, these unions stay silent to protect parent parties [6]. When national exams leaked, the ABVP defended Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan, triggering a major student backlash [6, 7]. Campus facilities are also crumbling. Under the Four Year Undergraduate Programme (FYUP), Delhi University doubled enrollment without building classrooms, leaving students jammed under cracked, falling ceilings [8].

### Creating a Non-Ideological Sanctuary
Students chose the CJP because it skipped rigid party lines [3]. Left-wing groups like AISA-SFI joined, but independent youth preferred the CJP banner to avoid partisan labels [3, 7]. Jantar Mantar became a safe haven. Muslim and Dalit youth, marginalized after earlier citizenship protests, found safety in the CJP's flat, decentralized structure [3].

## Basements, Death, and the Rajinder Nagar Tragedy

### Rau's IAS and Criminal Neglect
On July 27, 2024, heavy rain flooded the illegal basement library of Rau's IAS Study Circle in Old Rajinder Nagar, drowning three aspirants: Shreya Yadav, Tanya Soni, and Nivin Dalwin [9, 11]. The Delhi High Court blamed criminal neglect by city officials who ignored basic safety rules [9, 16]. The Court even mocked police for locking up a random SUV driver, Manuj Kathuria, because his car supposedly created a wave that broke the basement gate [16]. It handed the case to the CBI, while the MCD suspended an assistant engineer and fired a junior engineer in the Karol Bagh Zone [16, 28].

### Unregulated Housing and Rent Inflation
Rents are sky-high in student areas like Mukherjee Nagar, Laxmi Nagar, and Rajendra Nagar, forcing UPSC and SSC aspirants into hazardous spaces [12, 13]. Over two lakh paying guest (PG) hostels operate in Delhi without municipal registration or fire clearances [13]. Delhi Fire Department Director Atul Garg admitted his agency has zero data on these places [13]. Students live in windowless basements because they cost half as much as upstairs rooms, turning cheap housing into a death trap [12, 13].

## Paper Leaks: Breaking Point for Two Million Aspirants

### The NEET Leaks and Accountability
In May 2026, paper leaks in the NEET-UG medical exam ruined the dreams of two million aspirants and led to several student suicides [5]. When the government sat on its hands, students lost patience. Under the CJP banner, thousands marched through Delhi and clashed with police near Parliament, leaving over 100 injured [10]. They weren't trying to overthrow the government. They wanted one clear thing: the resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan [4, 10].

### Physical Protests and Backlash
On June 6, 2026, CJP co-founder Abhijeet Dipke returned from Boston University to lead Jantar Mantar protests, carrying B.R. Ambedkar's autobiography [5]. Protesters wore cockroach masks and carried heavy exam books to show who they were [10, 14]. On June 28, 2026, activist Sonam Wangchuk joined the sit-in on an indefinite hunger strike [6, 15]. But it wasn't all peaceful. In July 2026, a woman threw blue ink at Dipke during a press conference [15]. He shrugged it off by saying, 'Blue is my color,' referencing the blue flag of the Ambedkarite movement [5].

## Legislative Reform vs. Municipal Failure

### The Public Examinations Act and Guidelines
All this anger forced the federal government to act. Parliament passed the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act of 2024 to target cheating syndicates in national exams run by the NTA, UPSC, SSC, RRB, and IBPS [19, 22]. Under Section 10, organized cheating brings five to ten years in prison and a minimum ₹1 crore fine [24]. Individual offenses carry three to five years inside and a ₹10 lakh fine, while service providers face a ₹1 crore penalty and a four-year ban [23]. The Ministry of Education also issued 2024 guidelines banning coaching centers from enrolling kids under sixteen, with fines of ₹25,000 for a first violation and ₹1 lakh for the second [21, 25].

### Municipal Failure and Freebie Culture
But these laws stall at the municipal level. After the 2024 drownings, the MCD sealed thirteen illegal coaching basements in Karol Bagh [20]. To ease public anger, major coaching schools like Drishti IAS, Vajiram and Ravi, and IAS Gurukul offered ₹10 lakh to each victim's family, matching the ₹10 lakh paid by Delhi Lieutenant Governor Vinai Kumar Saxena [19, 26]. Sealing went on into June 2026, with the MCD shutting down thirty-six coaching properties [9, 27]. Delhi Education Minister Atishi announced a student-official committee to draft safety and rent regulations [17, 18]. Still, the Delhi High Court warned that city bodies remain bankrupt, blaming a political 'freebie culture' for starving the drainage budget of actual capital funds [11, 17, 18].
